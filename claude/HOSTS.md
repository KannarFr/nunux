# Home LAN machines: ocean and pacific

Two machines on the home LAN, reachable from this laptop as `ssh ocean` and `ssh pacific` (key-only). Addresses and user live in `~/.ssh/config` (the nunux `sshconfig`): always go through the alias, never a hardcoded IP. They are only reachable from the home network: away from it, assume they are not there. `ws1` is a different thing, see `WS1.md`.

**"Pacific" is also a Ceph release** (16.2.x, vs Reef 18.2.x). In Clever Cloud repos (axo, ovd, testcontainers-ceph), "Pacific" almost always means the Ceph track, not this Mac.

## ocean

Desktop PC, dual boot: Windows on its own disk, Arch Linux on a second disk (installed from WSL with `bin/wsl-install-arch` in the nunux repo, no LUKS so it comes back after a power cut). When it is booted on Arch, ssh works; when it is on Windows, it does not answer. As of 2026-10-11: 16 cores, 31G RAM, docker and claude installed, a copy of nunux in `~/git/kannar/nunux`. Re-check before relying on it (`ssh ocean 'nproc; free -g; command -v docker claude'`).

It is the user's own desktop, not a build server: do not start long jobs on it without asking. For offloaded work, `ws1` is the default.

**It also serves production**: `ai.myleapp.fr`, the LLM behind Myle's MyleAI assistant (Ollama in a docker container named `ollama` on the RTX 3080 Ti, behind nginx; setup in the myle repo's `local-ai/`). So rebooting it, booting it into Windows, restarting docker or nginx, or loading anything else onto its GPU takes MyleAI down or slows it, for real customers. Ask first. The model needs 7.6 of the card's 12 GB; past the rest, Ollama puts part of the model on the CPU and answers much more slowly.

It also runs **Home Assistant** (`~/homeassistant/compose.yaml`, container `homeassistant`, host networking, `http://ocean:8123`, or `http://ha.home` through the NAS's LAN proxy). Its LAN-only firewall rules are the `homeassistant:` lines in ocean's `/etc/nftables.conf` (8123/tcp, 1883/tcp MQTT, SSDP replies, Shelly CoIoT 5683/udp). It goes down with ocean. This repo is public, so what must not reach GitHub (how the wall screens log in, phone MACs, people's names) is in `~/.claude/HOSTS.private.md`, untracked and imported by `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` like this file: recreate it by hand on a rebuild. What to know before touching it:

- The site is a farm: HA floors are used as buildings (Maison, Ferme, Chez Louise, the sister's house next door, on the same UniFi network). The main dashboard is `/dashboard-ferme`. Electricity is EDF Tempo 18 kVA three-phase (6 kVA per phase); `config/packages/tempo.yaml` holds the Tempo colour, HC/HP period and kWh price sensors, and the prices are `input_number.tempo_prix_*` helpers.
- **Heating is central oil (fioul)**, not electric: the BLU/Wi-Fi TRVs only throttle hot water from the boiler, so Tempo prices have nothing to do with heating. Do not propose Tempo-driven heating; savings there are litres of fuel (lower setpoints at night, when nobody is home, windows open).
- The water heater (Shelly Pro 4PM in the cellier, output O3) is driven by a script on the Shelly itself ("Tempo + heures creuses"), which reapplies its state every 5 minutes: switching it from HA does not stick.
- Batteries: `config/packages/batteries.yaml` finds every battery sensor by device_class (counters, a "Batteries faibles" alert to `notify.foyer`); what to do per model is in `config/custom_templates/batteries.jinja`. The dashboard's Batteries view lists entities explicitly, so after adding a battery device re-run it: `docker exec -i -e HA_TOKEN="$(cat ~/homeassistant/claude.token)" homeassistant python - < ~/homeassistant/.ha-batteries-view.py` (the script lives on ocean's host, outside the container's `/config` mount, hence stdin). The `hors_ha_*_batterie` sensors are frozen Shelly-cloud readings (2026-10-12) for radiators HA could not reach: delete each one when its real device joins. The BLU gateways have HA's Bluetooth scanner on, which is how BTHome devices (weather station, BLU H&T) arrive. **BLU TRVs come through the gateway they are paired with in the Shelly app, not through BTHome**: each gateway's Shelly config entry carries its valves as sub-devices with a real `climate.*`. Three gateways: « GW BLE bureau » (…bbc8: Radiateur bureau), « BLE GW cuisine Nord » (…573c, added 2026-10-14: `climate.radiateur_salon_ouest`, `_salon_sud`, `_cheminee`, `_escalier`), « GW centre de conditionnement bureau » (…ad98: none). Their BTHome discoveries are ignored and the old BTHome entries for them deleted, so do not re-add a TRV through BTHome: it duplicates the battery. Gen1 Wi-Fi TRVs (`SHTRV-01`) are battery devices that sleep about an hour: HA can only add one while it is awake, and only if CoIoT is on and pointed at ocean (`/settings?coiot_enable=true&coiot_peer=10.0.0.151:5683`, then `/reboot`); with CoIoT off, the add fails with `cannot_connect` even though HTTP answers. Some bedroom valves are still outside HA (flat batteries), hence their `hors_ha_*` sensors; which ones is in `HOSTS.private.md`.
- Household alerts (Tempo colour, low batteries, washing machine done, close to tripping) go to `notify.foyer`, a notify group in `config/packages/foyer.yaml`; add a phone there by its `mobile_app_*` service. Members: the owner's iPhone and the other household member's Android phone (added 2026-10-14; service names in `HOSTS.private.md`). A new notify group only appears after an HA restart, not a reload. Two `script.sonner_*` scripts (one per phone, ids in `HOSTS.private.md`) ring a phone even on silent/DND (iOS critical alert; Android TTS on `alarm_stream_max`), every 10 s for a minute or until « Trouvé ! » is tapped; both wall-screen dashboards have buttons for them. Verified on the Android phone 2026-10-14. **Presence is Wi-Fi only, by the household's choice (2026-10-14): no GPS positions.** Each person's trackers are the UniFi clients of their phone, one per SSID since each SSID gets its own private MAC (the list is in `HOSTS.private.md`). A person is home when any of them is; the Companion apps' GPS trackers are disabled in HA. Do not re-enable them or add a GPS tracker to a person. A new SSID, or a phone switching its private address to "rotating", means a new MAC to add. Users: the owner (admin) and one non-admin household user.
- **The `http:` block in `configuration.yaml` is ignored** since HA 2026: HTTP settings (reverse-proxy trust for the NAS, 10.0.0.165) live in `config/.storage/http`. Edit that with HA stopped.
- Two Shelly Wall Displays (first-gen 4", `SAWD-0A1XX10EU1`), cuisine and escalier. Each shows its own dashboard (`/wallscreen-cuisine`, `/wallscreen-escalier`, storage mode, hidden from the sidebar; an open dashboard re-renders by itself when saved, so edit it through the API, no touch needed). How they log in, and their addresses, are in the untracked `HOSTS.private.md` (see the top of this section). The HA Shelly integration exposes each display's speaker as `media_player.shellywalldisplay_*`, and the display's local RPC (`http://<ip>/rpc/...`) can restart its app (`Sys.RestartApplication`). No usable microphone is confirmed on this model: ShellyElevate's voice satellite is only proven on the X2, and reverting it to stock is fragile (its issues #39, #92). `configuration.yaml` is root-owned: edit it through `docker exec -i homeassistant`.
- API access: a long-lived token in `~/homeassistant/claude.token` (mode 600; never print it, or `unifi.cred`, `unifi-protect.key`, `backup.key`, `mosquitto/*.password` next to it). The websocket API is reached with a small aiohttp script run inside the container (`docker exec -i -e HA_TOKEN=... homeassistant python -`).
- Backups: HA makes an encrypted backup daily at 04:15 (keeps 7, key in `backup.key`), and the user timer `ha-backup-copy.timer` rsyncs them at 05:00 to the NAS (`/share/homes/kannar/ha-backups/`) with `~/.ssh/ha-backup`, a key the NAS only accepts from ocean for that one rsync.

## pacific

Mac mini M2 (8 cores, 8G RAM, as of 2026-10-11), the iOS build machine for myle-mobile (`eas build --local`, Xcode, fastlane, CocoaPods). Known traps:

- Homebrew tools live in `/opt/homebrew/bin`, which a non-interactive ssh does not have on `PATH`: prefix remote commands with `export PATH=/opt/homebrew/bin:$PATH`, or everything looks uninstalled.
- Export `LANG=en_US.UTF-8` and `LC_ALL` in build commands, or CocoaPods exits 1.
- It has no GitHub key and cannot `git pull`: code is sent over with tar + scp.
- The disk is small (228G) and fills with builds; check free space (`ssh pacific 'df -h /'`) before an iOS build.

The full procedure is in the myle repo's `eas-local-deploy` skill and memory.
